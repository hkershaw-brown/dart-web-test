Sea Ice DA-Looza Brings the Community to Boulder

Sea ice is one of the most rapidly changing components of the Earth system, and one of the hardest to estimate. It drifts, ridges, melts and refreezes; it is observed by satellites, buoys and aircraft that each see a different part of the picture; and the quantities scientists care about most, such as ice concentration and thickness, are bounded and decidedly non-Gaussian. That makes sea ice a natural proving ground for new data assimilation methods.

From October 5–9, 2026, the Data Assimilation Research Section (DAReS) at the NSF National Center for Atmospheric Research (NSF NCAR) hosted the Sea Ice DA-Looza, a week-long working meeting in Boulder, Colorado, that brought together sea ice modelers, observationalists and data assimilation researchers to advance sea ice data assimilation with the Data Assimilation Research Testbed (DART).

The week was designed to mix the two halves of the problem. Mornings featured tutorials and science talks; afternoons were given over to hands-on software sessions where participants worked directly with DART developers.

Building a common foundation

The meeting opened on Monday with early-career participants picking up badges and office assignments before everyone gathered for welcome and introductions. Jeff Anderson, who leads DAReS, set the stage with an introduction to the ensemble algorithms at the heart of DART.

Marika Holland and Dave Bailey of NSF NCAR’s Climate and Global Dynamics Laboratory followed with an introduction to CICE, the sea ice model, and its role in the Community Earth System Model (CESM).
